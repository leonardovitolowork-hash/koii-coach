CHAPTER 10 / BUILD, LISTEN, RECORD, RELEASE

Cymatics house

Use your Cymatics House Starter Pack plus useful K.O. II factory sounds to build a danceable house track in 60 steps: 128 BPM, a four-bar groove and a 160-bar, five-minute arrangement.

Polished and release-ready is the goal, not a guarantee. This guide cannot promise an award, judge your mix or clear your rights, and it cannot see your pack or control your hardware. Song mode needs OS 2.0 or later; USB audio needs OS 2.5 or later.

Kick = deep thump. Clap = backbeat handclap. Closed hat = short ts. Open hat = longer tss. Stab = short chord. Riser = rising tension sound. Drop = full groove returns. A bar has four beats.

Browser storage is unavailable. The guide works, but progress and notes may not be saved.

Sample notebook

Write each imported library number and filename. A1 means group A, pad 1, not library number 1.

Editing helpers

Write a note: MAIN, group, stopped, − / + to the position, hold RECORD and tap the pad. Remove a whole pad track in a copied pattern: MAIN, group, stopped, hold ERASE + the pad until TRK blinks, then release. For one note, navigate to it and briefly press ERASE + pad.

Never use ERASE + SOUND to fix a sequence: it permanently deletes audio. Copy scenes before editing them and re-check the source afterwards. Pad sound settings and group volume are shared by all scenes.

01 / Set up and choose sounds
  1. Back up first. Stop playback. Keep your original Cymatics download. Use the official EP Sample Tool to save a local backup before changing the sound library. A scene COMMIT is not an external backup.
  2. Open a spare project. Press MAIN, hold MAIN and tap an unused project pad 1–9. Check groups A–D are empty. If any music matters, pick another project; never clear one for this guide.
  3. Set 128 BPM. TEMPO, turn X to 128. TIMING, turn X to 16 (not 16T) with neutral swing. Press MAIN. This guide assumes 4/4.
  4. Pick Cymatics sounds. Unzip the House Starter Pack and audition the audio files only. Pick one kick, one clap, one short bass one-shot and one chord, stab or musical loop. Choose by role; the guide cannot see your filenames.
  5. Pick support sounds. Choose a closed hat, an open hat and quiet percussion from Cymatics or the K.O. II factory sounds, plus an optional riser and impact. Check key labels so bass and hook match. Keep the palette small.
02 / Load the sounds
  1. Connect. Power on, connect with a USB-C data cable, open the official EP Sample Tool in a compatible browser and allow the device connection. Stay out of SAMPLE mode.
  2. Protect slots. Save and verify a backup first. Note which library slots are used. Library sounds are shared by every project, so deleting or replacing one can break other music.
  3. Upload a few sounds. Upload only your chosen files into unused slots, wait for the transfers to finish, then audition each one in the tool.
  4. Write down numbers. Record each library number and filename in the notebook below. A1 means group A, pad 1; it is not library number 1.
  5. Check on the device. SOUND, group A, pad 1; hold SOUND and type the kick library number on the pads. Tap pad 1. Uploading never builds your pad layout for you.
03 / Assign and trim
  1. Group A. A1 kick, A2 clap, A3 closed hat, A4 open hat, A5 percussion. For each: SOUND, pick the pad, hold SOUND, type the library number. Audition factory sounds instead of trusting number ranges.
  2. Groups B, C and D. B1 bass one-shot; C1 hook; C2 optional short answer; D1 riser; D2 impact; D3 atmosphere. Leave unused pads empty.
  3. Trim safely. Pick the sound, tap SHIFT + SOUND, use − / + to find TRIM. X sets the start, Y the length. Cut silence and long tails, not the attack. Never hold SHIFT + SOUND in TRIM: it permanently crops the file.
  4. Level and playback. In SOUND, X is level and Y is pitch. Keep the original pitch for bass and hook. Use oneshot playback for this recipe and keep kick and bass centered.
  5. Time a loop. If C1 is a loop: SHIFT + SOUND, TIME. Set its ORIGINAL BPM or its real bar length, then listen at 128 BPM. If it sounds rough, switch to a short stab.
04 / One-bar foundation
  1. Note entry. MAIN, group A, stopped. Use − / + to reach a position such as 1.2.3 (bar 1, beat 2, third sixteenth). Hold RECORD, tap the pad, release. A brief ERASE + pad removes one wrong hit; never ERASE + SOUND.
  2. Kicks. Write A1 at 1.1.1, 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1: one kick on every beat. Press PLAY to hear it, then stop.
  3. Claps. Write A2 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1. You should hear kick, kick + clap, kick, kick + clap.
  4. Hats. Write A3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3, the gaps between kicks. A closed hat is the short ts sound.
  5. Balance. In SOUND set X per pad: kick strongest, clap clear, hats lower. Avoid distortion. Stop and press MAIN.
05 / Four-bar groove
  1. Extend to four bars. Group A stopped: tap RECORD, hold SHIFT and press + once (two bars) and once more (four bars), then tap RECORD to disarm. Confirm the length is four bars.
  2. Hat accents. Write A3 at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4. Erase just those two if it feels busy.
  3. Open hats. At 2.4.3 and 4.4.3 briefly erase A3, then write A4. Shorten the tail of A4 if it covers the next kick.
  4. Percussion. Write A5 at 2.3.4 and 4.3.4, quietly. Pick another sound later if it clashes with the hook.
  5. Turnaround. Write a quiet A2 pickup at 4.4.4. Listen into bar 1 and remove it if it rushes.
06 / Bassline
  1. Four-bar B. MAIN, B, stopped: tap RECORD, set the pattern to four bars with − / +, tap RECORD to disarm.
  2. Bar one. Write B1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.2, 1.3.3 and 1.4.2, between the kicks.
  3. Bars two and three. Repeat at 2.1.3, 2.2.2, 2.3.3, 2.4.2 and 3.1.3, 3.2.2, 3.3.3, 3.4.2. Check you are still in group B.
  4. Bar four gap. Write B1 at 4.1.3, 4.2.3 and 4.3.2 and leave beat 4 empty. Shorten the bass tail if it rings through.
  5. Check the low end. Lower B1 or shorten its tail if the kick disappears. Check on headphones and a bigger speaker; the built-in speaker hides bass.
07 / Cymatics hook
  1. Four-bar C and D. Set groups C and D to four bars the same way. Recheck that A and B are four bars.
  2. Place the hook. Short stab: C1 at 1.1.3 and 3.1.3. Four-bar loop: C1 at 1.1.1 only. Two-bar loop: 1.1.1 and 3.1.1. One-bar loop: 1.1.1, 2.1.1, 3.1.1 and 4.1.1. Use one method only.
  3. Optional answer. Write C2 at 2.4.3 and 4.3.3 if it is a short sound that fits. Remove or shorten it if it clashes.
  4. Keep the hook clear. Play A, B and C together. Lower the hook until the drums stay clear and keep the answer quieter. If bass and hook clash, choose a steadier stab.
  5. Check cutoffs. If sounds cut off, remove overlapping layers and long tails. Keep D silent in the main groove; transitions come later.
08 / Scenes
  1. Name DROP A. Stop on the finished groove and write its scene number in the notebook. This is DROP A.
  2. DROP B. On DROP A press SHIFT + MAIN to COMMIT a copy and note its new number as DROP B. Add A5 at 4.1.4. Return to DROP A and check it did not change.
  3. GROOVE. COMMIT a copy of DROP A. In the copy, erase the C2 and A5 tracks (helper above). Keep drums, bass and C1. Note it as GROOVE.
  4. INTRO. COMMIT a copy of GROOVE. Erase the B1 and C1 tracks and A4. Keep A1, A2 and A3. Note it as INTRO.
  5. OUTRO. COMMIT a copy of INTRO and note it as OUTRO. Erase the extra hats at 2.2.4 and 4.2.4 and the pickup at 4.4.4. Re-check that DROP A and GROOVE are unchanged.
09 / Breakdown and build
  1. BREAK. COMMIT a copy of DROP A and note it as BREAK. Erase A1, A2, A4, A5 and B1 (and C2 if busy). Keep quiet A3 and C1. Optional D3 at 1.1.1.
  2. BUILD start. COMMIT a copy of BREAK and note it as BUILD. Bass stays out. Write A2 at 1.2.1, 1.4.1; 2.1.1, 2.2.1, 2.3.1, 2.4.1; then 3.1.1, 3.1.3, 3.2.1, 3.2.3, 3.3.1, 3.3.3, 3.4.1, 3.4.3.
  3. BUILD finish. Write A2 at 4.1.1, 4.1.3, 4.2.1, 4.2.3, 4.3.1 and 4.3.3 and leave beat 4 empty. Optional D1 riser at 1.1.1 only if it fits four bars.
  4. Silence before the drop. On beat 4 of bar 4 erase any A3, C1, C2 or D3 hit and shorten tails that cross the scene end. The gap makes the drop hit harder.
  5. FINAL. COMMIT a copy of DROP B and note it as FINAL. Optionally write a quiet D2 impact at 1.1.1 if it does not hide the kick. FINAL plays once, then DROP B returns.
10 / Arrange 160 bars
  1. Check numbers. Fill in all eight scene numbers below. Every scene must have a longest pattern of exactly four bars. 40 positions × 4 bars = 160 bars = 5:00 at 128 BPM.
  2. Open the song list. Needs OS 2.0 or later. Stop, hold MAIN and press ENTER. Use − / + to move between positions and SHIFT + − / + to pick the scene number. Position 1 is not scene 1.
  3. First eight positions. Positions 1–4 INTRO, 5–8 GROOVE. SHIFT + A adds a position. Repeating a scene number repeats its four bars, so no extra copies are needed.
  4. Remaining positions. Follow the table: 9–16 DROP A; 17–24 DROP B; 25–28 BREAK; 29–30 BUILD; 31 FINAL; 32–36 DROP B; 37–40 OUTRO.
  5. Play it through. In the song list press SHIFT + PLAY. BREAK starts at bar 97, BUILD at 113, FINAL at 121 and OUTRO at 145. Fix rough transitions scene by scene.
11 / Mix and check
  1. Balance. SOUND, pad, X sets each level: kick first, then bass, clap, hats, hook and FX. Holding a group button and moving the fader changes that whole group in every scene.
  2. Effects. Use FX for a little reverb or delay on the hook; the effect is shared, not per pad. Keep kick and bass mostly dry.
  3. Optional ducking. On OS 2.0 or later: SHIFT + FX, then + for sidechain. Hold A and pick pad 1 as the source; hold SHIFT + B for the destination. Start gently so the bass dips briefly.
  4. Compare. Play DROP B and FINAL beside a house track you like at the same volume. Fix harsh hats, clicks, muddy bass and a lost hook first.
  5. Quality gate. Listen start to finish on headphones, a speaker and quietly. Kick and bass distinct, hook memorable, break emptier, drops danceable. Fix the three biggest problems, then record.
12 / Record and publish
  1. Choose a route. OS 2.5 or later: USB audio into a compatible computer and recorder. Otherwise 3.5 mm stereo output into audio interface line inputs. Backups and COMMIT are not audio exports.
  2. Record. Arm a stereo track, avoid feedback loops and keep peaks below clipping (around −6 dBFS is a good start). Start recording, then SHIFT + PLAY in the song list. Capture the final tail.
  3. Finish a copy. Keep the raw recording. Trim silence, fix clicks, add gentle EQ or compression if needed and make a separate mastered copy. Do not chase one loudness number.
  4. Export and check. Export a lossless stereo WAV at the recorded sample rate, in the format your distributor asks for. Listen to the whole file for clipping, missing channels and abrupt endings.
  5. Rights and release. Keep your Cymatics license for this pack. Check vocals from other sources separately. Do not share raw samples. Check your distributor Content ID rules, then submit title, credits, artwork and WAV. No award is promised.
