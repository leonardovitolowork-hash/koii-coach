CHAPTER 10 / THE LEGEND PLAN

Cymatics house: the legend plan

Use your Cymatics House Starter Pack plus K.O. II factory sounds and your own voice to build a 160-bar, five-minute, 128 BPM house record designed to be remembered: a hook people can hum, a groove that moves a room, a kick that makes the music breathe, a filter that tells a story, a human voice and drops that land because of what came before.

Aiming for legendary is a direction, not a guarantee. No guide can promise an award or a classic: that depends on your hook, your ears, your mix and your release. The controls here were checked against the official K.O. II documentation and trusted manuals, but your OS version may label things slightly differently, so trust the official links at the bottom.

Eight rules of the record

  1. One hook, simple enough to hum
  2. Groove first, then everything else
  3. The kick makes the music breathe (sidechain)
  4. The filter tells the story
  5. Build the fullest scene, then subtract
  6. One beat of silence before the drop
  7. A human voice and one fingerprint only you have
  8. Fresh ears before you finish

Learn from the greats: the filtered-loop and sidechain-pump sound of French house, and the late-90s habit of building a track around one memorable loop. Use only sounds you are allowed to use: your pack, the factory library and your own voice.

How long this takes

Following every step means about 120 hand-entered notes, plus copying and erasing. Plan three sessions: (1) chapters 1–5, groove, bass and hook, about two hours; (2) chapters 6–9, voice, scenes, filters and arrangement, about two hours; (3) chapters 10–12 on another day, mix, tests and release. A rested ear in session 3 is part of the method.

Words used here. Group: one of four banks of pads, A to D; each group has its own pattern. Pattern: the notes of one group. Scene: one saved combination of the four group patterns. COMMIT: press SHIFT + MAIN to save a copy of the current scene, then edit the copy. Kick = deep thump. Clap = backbeat handclap. Ghost hat = very quiet short hat. Open hat = longer tss. Sidechain = the kick makes other sounds duck. Filter sweep = opening or closing the brightness. Drop = full groove returns. A bar has four beats. Positions read bar.beat.sixteenth, so 1.2.3 is bar 1, beat 2, third sixteenth.

Pad layout (the kick lives alone in group A)

Group A: A1 kick. Group B: B1 clap, B2 ghost hat, B3 open hat, B4 percussion, B5 riser, B6 impact, B7 atmosphere. Group C: C1 bass. Group D: D1 hook, D2 pad, D3 voice, D4 second voice take. The sidechain trigger is a whole group, so only the kick is allowed in group A. Groups C and D are ducked; group B is not.

KEYS pad map (minor pentatonic)

Pads run low to high: . 0 ENTER 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9. With the minor pentatonic scale from step 4: pad 1 = root, 2 = minor third, 3 = fourth, 4 = fifth, 5 = flat seventh, 6 = root an octave up. Below the root: ENTER = flat seventh, 0 = fifth, . = fourth. Every pad is in key. Pad 1 plays a sample at the pitch it was recorded at; the scale only sets the steps above and below it. If bass and hook clash, hold KEYS and tap another pad to move the root until they agree.

Sample notebook

Write each imported library number and filename. A1 means group A, pad 1, not library number 1.

Editing helpers

Write a note: MAIN, stopped, SHIFT + − jumps to 1.1.1, − / + moves, hold RECORD and tap the pad. Erase one note: navigate to it and briefly press ERASE + pad. Erase a whole pad track in a copied pattern: hold ERASE and the pad until TRK blinks. Undo: stop playback, then SHIFT + B.

Never use ERASE + SOUND to fix a sequence: it permanently deletes audio. COMMIT gives the copy its own patterns, but never change a group's pattern number with − / + while editing a copy: that would link two scenes so edits hit both. Re-check the source scene after each edit. Pad sound settings and group volume are shared by all scenes.

01 / Foundation: protect, tempo, scale, hook-first
  1. Back up first. Stop playback. Keep your original Cymatics download. Use the official EP Sample Tool to save a local backup before changing the sound library. A scene COMMIT is not an external backup.
  2. Open a spare project. Press MAIN, hold MAIN and tap an unused project pad 1–9. Check groups A–D are empty. If any music matters, pick another project; never clear one for this guide.
  3. Set 128 BPM and the grid. TEMPO, turn X to 128. TIMING, turn X to 16 (1/16), then press − for quantized recording. Leave swing alone until step 15. Press MAIN.
  4. Set the minor pentatonic scale. SHIFT + ERASE opens system settings. Use − / + to reach PAD, press ENTER, choose the scale option and press ENTER again, then pick minor pentatonic (system code 319, if you prefer typing it). Press MAIN. Now every KEYS pad lands in key. If your hook sample sounds major and bright, use major pentatonic (318) instead. Check by ear that the scale really changed.
  5. Choose the hook before anything else. Audition the Cymatics melodic one-shots (pluck, stab, vocal ah). Pick ONE short, bright, pitched sound you could imagine as a four-note tune, and a bass one-shot labelled with the same key. Then pick a tight kick, clap, quiet closed hat, open hat, percussion, pad, riser, impact and atmosphere. Choose by role; the guide cannot see your filenames.
02 / Load and assign the sounds
  1. Connect and protect. Power on, connect with a USB-C data cable, open the official EP Sample Tool in a compatible browser and allow the device connection. Save and verify a backup. Note which library slots are used; library sounds are shared by every project.
  2. Upload and write numbers. Upload only your chosen files into unused slots, wait for transfers to finish and audition each one. Write each library number and filename in the notebook below. A1 means group A, pad 1; it is not library number 1.
  3. Assign groups A and B. Group A holds only the kick: A1. It becomes the trigger for the pump later. Group B: B1 clap, B2 ghost hat, B3 open hat, B4 percussion, B5 riser, B6 impact, B7 atmosphere. For each: SOUND, pick the group and pad, type the library number on the pads and press ENTER. Tap the pad to check it.
  4. Assign groups C and D. Group C: C1 bass. Group D: D1 hook, D2 pad or chord, D3 vocal chop (you add it in chapter 6). Same method: SOUND, group, pad, library number, ENTER. Leave other pads empty.
  5. Trim the sounds. Pick a sound, press SHIFT + SOUND and use − / + to find TRIM. X sets the start, Y the length. Cut silence and long tails, not the attack. Keep the kick short so the bass fits and the open hat about an eighth note long. Do not hold SHIFT + SOUND for two seconds; that saves the edit to the sound itself.
  6. Set the levels. Back in SOUND, X sets each pad level. Kick strongest, clap clear, B2 ghost hat very quiet. Tap the pads one after another and compare.
03 / The groove (this decides everything)
  1. Learn note entry. MAIN, stopped. Press SHIFT + − to jump to 1.1.1, then use − / + to move step by step. Hold RECORD and tap a pad to write a note. At a step, a brief ERASE + pad removes that note. Stop playback before undoing: SHIFT + B undoes the last action.
  2. Kick and clap. Group A: write A1 at 1.1.1, 1.2.1, 1.3.1 and 1.4.1, one kick on every beat. Group B: write B1 at 1.2.1 and 1.4.1. Press PLAY to hear one bar, then stop.
  3. Offbeat open hat and ghost hats. Group B: write B3 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3, the gap between kicks. Write B2 at 1.1.4, 1.2.4, 1.3.4 and 1.4.4 as quiet ghost notes just before each kick.
  4. Add swing. TIMING, then turn Y a little. The ghost hats should skip, not stumble. Stop as soon as it feels alive. If kick and clap seem late, reduce it.
  5. Extend to eight bars. Pattern length belongs to the whole group, so do this for group A and again for group B: tap RECORD to arm, hold SHIFT and press + (two bars), again (four bars), again (eight bars), then tap RECORD to disarm. Confirm each group shows eight bars.
  6. Groove test. Loop the eight bars quietly, stand up and nod. If it does not already make you move, fix it now: ghost hat level, open hat length, swing, kick tail. Do not add more sounds to hide a weak groove.
04 / Bass and the pump
  1. Find the bass note. MAIN, group C, select C1, press KEYS. The pads become a keyboard. Hold KEYS and press − until the bass is low and warm. Pad 1 is the root. Tap pads to hear it; every pad is in key.
  2. Write the offbeat bass. Group C, stopped, pattern at 1 bar. In KEYS mode hold RECORD and press pad 1 at 1.1.3, 1.2.3, 1.3.3 and 1.4.3. The bass answers the kick instead of fighting it.
  3. Extend and add movement. Tap RECORD, hold SHIFT and press + three times (1, 2, 4, 8 bars), disarm. In bar 4 erase the note at 4.4.3 and write the pad just below pad 1 (the ENTER pad) there. In bar 8 erase 8.3.3 and write the ENTER pad there.
  4. Set up the sidechain. Press SHIFT + FX to enter output mode, then press PLUS to activate sidechain. Hold group pad A: it is the trigger, which is why the kick lives alone in group A. Hold SHIFT and press group pad C to duck the bass. Later add group D the same way. X sets the duck length, Y the shape.
  5. Tune the pump. Play groups A, B and C. Turn X until the bass ducks on every kick and is back before the next one. Use Y for a smooth curve. Too deep weakens the groove; too shallow loses the pump.
  6. Check kick and bass. Listen quietly. Kick and bass must stay distinct. If they blur, lower the C1 level with SOUND X or shorten its tail with SHIFT + SOUND, TRIM. Never fix a muddy low end by turning the master up.
05 / The hook (this is the record)
  1. Find the hook pad. Group D, select D1, press KEYS and hold KEYS + − or + until the hook sits bright and clear. Tap pads 1–9 to hear the scale. Set the group D pattern to two bars: tap RECORD, press + once, disarm.
  2. Write the eight-note phrase. Hold RECORD and press: pad 1 at 1.1.3; pad 2 at 1.2.2; pad 1 at 1.2.4; pad 4 at 1.3.3; pad 3 at 2.1.3; pad 2 at 2.2.2; pad 1 at 2.3.3; the ENTER pad at 2.4.2. That is root, up a step, back, up to the fifth, then a falling answer.
  3. Run the hum test. Loop two bars with the drums. After three listens, hum it. If you cannot, delete notes: the strongest hooks are simple. Never add notes to rescue a weak hook; pick a better sound instead.
  4. Extend to eight bars. Tap RECORD, hold SHIFT and press + twice (four bars, then eight bars), disarm. The phrase now repeats four times.
  5. Write the question and the answer. Bar 4: erase the copied notes at 4.1.3, 4.2.2, 4.3.3 and 4.4.2, then write pad 5 at 4.1.3, pad 4 at 4.2.2 and pad 3 at 4.3.3 (it rises and does not land). Bar 8: erase its copied notes at 8.1.3, 8.2.2, 8.3.3 and 8.4.2, then write the ENTER pad at 8.1.3 and pad 1 at 8.2.3 (it lands home).
  6. Give the hook a filter. Select group D. Hold FADER and pick the low-pass filter function from the labels above the pads. Move the fader and listen: the hook should open and close. Leave it fully open. Add group D to the sidechain destinations now if you have not.
06 / The human voice and your fingerprint
  1. Write the words. A voice is what turns a great beat into a song. Choose 2–4 syllables that end on an open vowel and mean something to you, in plain words you invent (for example a short promise or a one-word cheer). Speak them on the beat at 128 BPM until they swing. Never use a famous lyric.
  2. Record your voice. Press SAMPLE and choose the microphone as input. X sets the input level and Y the threshold: set the threshold above room noise so recording starts when you sing. Hum the bass root note first so your voice lands near the key. Hold pad D3, sing, then release. If the built-in speaker feeds back, turn monitoring off. Record 5–10 dry takes; length is limited, so keep them short.
  3. Trim the best syllable. Pick the best take, press SHIFT + SOUND and trim the strongest syllable to 200–300 ms. Cut breaths and clicks. Write its library number and your words in the notebook.
  4. Tune the voice to the hook. Pad 1 plays a sample at the pitch it was recorded at, so match by ear. Use the pitch setting in SOUND (one semitone per step) and, for fine steps, the tune function on the fader (the options are printed above the pads). Stop when the voice on pad 1 and the hook on pad 1 sound like the same note. Do not skip this.
  5. Play it as a riff. Select D3 and press KEYS. Use pad 4 for every voice note: it agrees with every hook note it overlaps. Do not use pad 6 for the voice: +12 semitones halves its length and sounds cartoonish. Pitching up shortens a note; pitching down lengthens it.
  6. Set the level. Start the voice pad level (SOUND X) lower than the hook and raise it until the words are clear. Keep it fairly dry; a small reverb send on group D adds air. The group D filter sweeps will open and close the voice with the hook.
  7. Choose one fingerprint. Pick ONE thing only your track has: your voice an octave lower under the hook in the breaks. For that, trim a copy of the take to 200 ms or less before pitching it down, because pitching down doubles its length. You place it in chapter 7. Write your choice in the notebook.
  8. Check the rights of the voice. Your own voice is yours. A hired singer needs a signed agreement. A sample from a record or a vinyl needs clearance for both the recording and the song before release. Read the vinyl card below before you decide.
07 / Scenes: build the fullest, then subtract
  1. Lock DROP 1. Play groups A, B, C and D together for eight bars. It should groove, pump and hook. Write its scene number in the notebook, then press SHIFT + MAIN to COMMIT a copy. Do not continue until this alone is exciting.
  2. Make DROP 1+. In the copy: write B4 at 2.3.4, 4.3.4, 6.3.4, 8.3.4 and 8.4.2. Write D2 at 1.1.1, 3.1.1, 5.1.1 and 7.1.1 (the group pattern is already eight bars). Note the new scene number as DROP 1+.
  3. Make DROP 2. COMMIT a copy of DROP 1+. Add B7 at 1.1.1 and your voice D3 (pad 4) at 2.4.4, 4.4.4, 6.4.4 and 8.4.4. Note the new scene number as DROP 2. Never put the voice on a kick step: the pump ducks it.
  4. Make DROP 2+. COMMIT a copy of DROP 2. Add D3 (pad 4) at 1.4.4, 3.4.4, 5.4.4 and 7.4.4, a B1 clap fill at 4.4.3, 4.4.4, 8.4.2, 8.4.3 and 8.4.4, and a once-only voice fill on KEYS pad 3 at 8.3.4. Note it as DROP 2+.
  5. Make FINAL DROP. Select DROP 2 again (not DROP 2+) and COMMIT a copy. Add B6 at 1.1.1. Note it as FINAL DROP. Check where the new scene landed and that no scene you want was overwritten; if it was, stop and recreate it from its source.
  6. Make the opening scenes. COMMIT a copy of DROP 1 and erase the C1 track: INTRO2 (kick, clap, hats, hook teaser). COMMIT a copy of INTRO2 and erase B1 and D1: INTRO1. COMMIT a copy of DROP 1+ and erase D1 and D2: GROOVE2. COMMIT a copy of GROOVE2 and erase B4: GROOVE1. To erase a whole pad track, hold ERASE and the pad until TRK blinks.
  7. Make the quiet scenes. COMMIT a copy of DROP 1+ and erase A1, B1–B4 and C1. Add B7 at 1.1.1 and your fingerprint: D3 an octave lower (the 200 ms copy, hold KEYS + −) on pad 1 at 1.1.1, 3.1.1, 5.1.1 and 7.1.1. Note it as BREAK1. COMMIT a copy of BREAK1 and add B5 at 5.1.1: BREAK2.
  8. Strip the build. COMMIT a copy of DROP 1. Erase C1. Erase B3 and B2 from bar 7 onward and erase the A1 kicks at 8.3.1 and 8.4.1. Erase the whole B1 track (hold ERASE and the pad until TRK blinks): you rewrite it as the roll in the next step.
  9. Write the roll. Write B1 as the roll: bars 1–2 beats 2 and 4; bars 3–4 every beat; bars 5–6 every eighth; bar 7 every sixteenth; bar 8 the first twelve sixteenths. Add B5 at 5.1.1. Note this scene as BUILD1. Then COMMIT a copy, add B7 at 1.1.1 and note it as BUILD2.
  10. Make the way out. COMMIT a copy of GROOVE2 and erase C1 and B4: OUTRO1. COMMIT a copy of OUTRO1 and erase B1 and B3: OUTRO2. Re-check DROP 1 is unchanged.
08 / The filter journey
  1. Teaser in INTRO2. Select INTRO2 and group D. Hold FADER and pick the low-pass filter function. Start playback with the fader at the closed end, hold RECORD and nudge it open very slowly over the eight bars. Release RECORD. The hook should only hint.
  2. Opening the break. In BREAK1 record a slow sweep from closed to half open. In BREAK2 record from half open to fully open across eight bars.
  3. Sweeping the builds. In BUILD1 and BUILD2 record the sweep from nearly closed to fully open across the eight bars, finishing exactly as the scene ends.
  4. Check the drops. Play every drop scene: the hook must be fully open and bright. If it is muffled, select group D, hold ERASE + FADER while it plays to clear stray fader moves, and re-check.
09 / Arrange 160 bars
  1. Confirm scene numbers. Fill in all fifteen scene numbers below. Every group in a scene must be exactly eight bars. 20 positions × 8 bars = 160 bars = 5:00 at 128 BPM.
  2. Open the song list. Needs OS 2.0 or later. Stop, hold MAIN and press ENTER. − / + moves between positions. SHIFT + A adds a position and SHIFT + − / + chooses its scene. Position 1 is not scene 1.
  3. Enter positions 1–10. 1 INTRO1, 2 INTRO2, 3 GROOVE1, 4 GROOVE2, 5 BUILD1, 6 DROP 1, 7 DROP 1+, 8 DROP 1, 9 BREAK1, 10 BREAK2. Use the actual numbers from the notebook.
  4. Enter positions 11–20. 11 BUILD2, 12 FINAL DROP, 13 DROP 2, 14 DROP 2+, 15 DROP 2, 16 DROP 2+, 17 GROOVE2, 18 OUTRO1, 19 OUTRO2, 20 OUTRO2.
  5. Play it through. Press SHIFT + PLAY to start from the beginning. Listen without editing: the first drop lands at bar 41, the break at bar 65, the final drop at bar 89. Note rough seams, then fix only the scene concerned.
10 / Mix like a record, not a loop
  1. Set the level order. Balance in this order: kick, bass, clap, hats, hook, pad, voice, FX. SOUND X sets each pad. Holding a group button and moving the fader sets that whole group in every scene. The riser and impact sit under the kick, not over it. A drop must feel bigger because of what came before it.
  2. Re-tune the pump. In DROP 2+ re-check the sidechain: groups C and D should both breathe with the kick without disappearing.
  3. Use one effect. The K.O. II has one shared effect. Press FX and choose a small reverb or short delay, then set each group send. Put a little on the hook, pad and voice and almost none on kick and bass. Wash makes a hook forgettable.
  4. Protect the headroom. Keep the K.O. II output below clipping: lower group volumes (hold the group and move the fader) if it distorts. Leave compression and limiting to the finishing step.
  5. Compare to a reference. Play a house record you love at the same volume. Compare kick weight, bass, hook brightness, voice and hat level. Adjust one thing at a time toward it.
  6. Check small speakers and mono. Play it quietly, then on a phone speaker. The bass should still be audible through its harmonics and the kick should not vanish. Later, check the recording in mono.
11 / The legend tests
  1. Ten-second test. Jump to a random bar. Within ten seconds it must feel like a dance record, not a loop.
  2. Hum test. Play the first drop once and then stop it. Can you hum the hook? If not, simplify it or replace the sound.
  3. Drop test. Does the first drop feel bigger than the build before it? If not: lower the riser, deepen the subtraction and keep the one-beat gap before the drop.
  4. Phone and stranger test. Play it on a phone speaker, then for someone who has not heard it. Watch their face in the first drop. Fix anything that disappears or confuses.
  5. Next-day test. Listen tomorrow with fresh ears and fix only the three biggest problems. More tweaks usually hurt.
12 / Record, finish and release
  1. Choose a route. OS 2.5 or later: USB audio into a compatible computer and recorder. Otherwise 3.5 mm stereo output into audio interface line inputs. Backups and COMMIT are not audio exports.
  2. Record the pass. Arm a stereo track, avoid feedback loops and keep peaks below clipping (around −6 dBFS is a good start). Start recording, then SHIFT + PLAY. For a live feel you can hold FX and press pads for punch-in effects at transitions; keep a clean take as well.
  3. Finish a copy. Keep the raw recording. Trim silence, fix clicks and make a separate mastered copy with gentle EQ, compression and a true-peak limiter if needed. Compare to your reference at the same volume and do not chase one loudness number.
  4. Export and check. Export a lossless stereo WAV at the recorded sample rate in the format your distributor asks for. Listen to the whole file for clipping, missing channels and abrupt endings.
  5. Make the 20-second teaser. Cut a 20-second clip that starts eight beats before the first drop and runs through the first bar of the drop. Use it for a short video or social post. The drop is your hook, so show it first.
  6. Rights and release. Keep your Cymatics license for this pack. Keep proof of your own voice recording. Never release a recognisable sample from a record without clearance. Check your distributor Content ID rules, then submit title, credits, artwork and WAV. No award is promised.

Using a vinyl voice (Santana, Dire Straits): read this first

This is general information, not legal advice. Verdict: a voice from your vinyls is fine for a private DJ edit or practice, but do not release a recognisable sample without permission.

The rule. In the EU, the court of justice held in 2019 that taking even a very short sample from a recording can infringe the record producer's right, unless it is used in a modified form unrecognisable to the ear. The song itself, meaning the writer and publisher, is a separate right. For a release you need permission for both the recording and the composition, in writing.

My picks, for a private edit or if you will clear it. First choice: Santana, Oye Como Va (disc 1, track 7): the title words as a one- or two-syllable chop. Backup: Dire Straits, Sultans of Swing (side 2, track 1): the single word swing, which suits a swung groove. I cannot hear your pressings, so choose the cleanest moment: the voice alone, no loud instruments, a short gap before and after.

How to capture it. Turntable, then a phono preamp (unless your turntable has one built in), then the K.O. II 3.5 mm stereo line input. Clean the record and set the level so the peaks never clip. Press SAMPLE as in step 31 but choose the line input, record two or three seconds around the phrase, trim it, tune it as in step 33 and place it on D3 instead of your own voice. Name the project PRIVATE EDIT.

How to release it. Identify who owns the recording (usually a label) and who owns the composition (usually a publisher), send each a short request describing your track and what you used, and wait for two signed agreements. Famous voices are caught automatically by Content ID, and pitching or filtering a famous phrase rarely makes it unrecognisable.
